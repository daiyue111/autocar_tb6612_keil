# MSPM0G3507 Competition Car Template

This project keeps the proven line-following car available while adding a
fixed-period, encoder-based three-loop platform for competition tasks.

## Runtime Profiles

Select one profile in `app_config.h`:

- `APP_MODE_SAFE_IDLE`: all motors and the MOSFET load switch stay off.
- `APP_MODE_ENCODER_DIAGNOSTIC`: motors stay off while wheel encoder wiring,
  direction, and counts are checked.
- `APP_MODE_SPEED_TUNE`: reserved for guarded wheel-speed PI calibration.
- `APP_MODE_LINE_GYRO`: proven 8-channel line following with gyro heading
  hold after line loss. This is the safe default.
- `APP_MODE_SQUARE_3LOOP`: 2025 E square-field mission with line control,
  encoder speed/distance control, and IMU turn control.
- `APP_MODE_CHASSIS_CALIBRATION`: three-stage on-track calibration for wheel
  distance scale, effective skid-steer track, and corner-center offset.

Diagnostic, speed-tune, calibration, line-following, and square-task profiles
are compiled during verification. The final HEX is currently left in
`APP_MODE_SQUARE_3LOOP`; PA09 starts the square mission and a second press
stops it.

In `APP_MODE_SPEED_TUNE`, keep the chassis on a stand and press PA09 to start
or stop both wheel-speed loops. Encoder loss, reversed feedback, or excessive
measured speed stops both motors. `gMotionFault` values are: 0 healthy, 1/2
left/right no feedback, 3/4 left/right reversed feedback, and 5/6 left/right
overspeed.

## Architecture

- Application: `empty_mspm0g3507.c`, `car_app.c/.h`, `app_config.h`
- Missions: `square_mission.c/.h`, `chassis_calibration.c/.h`
- Control: `line_follow.c/.h`, `line_speed_control.c/.h`,
  `motion_control.c/.h`, `pid.c/.h`
- Platform: `control_scheduler.c/.h`
- Chassis model: `chassis_config.h`, `chassis_model.c/.h`,
  `calibration_store.c/.h`
- Drivers: `motor`, `encoder`, `track`, `imu`, `power_switch`, and `app_io`
- Generated: SysConfig, MCU initialization, and startup code

The main loop is scheduled by SysTick at 1 ms. Motor PWM is generated by
TIMA0 and TIMG6 hardware, so PID execution is not blocked by software PWM.
The last 1 KB main-Flash sector at `0x0001FC00` is excluded from the linker
image and holds one checksummed chassis-calibration record.

## Empirical Chassis Calibration

The current calibration profile uses PA09 for every confirmation and PA00 as
its only required LED. Three quick flashes identify the calibration firmware
at boot. Ready-state pulses are 0.5 seconds on and 0.5 seconds off: one pulse
per group means stage 1 ready, two pulses means stage 2 ready, and three
pulses means stage 3 ready. Solid on means the car can move, a 2-second pulse
means complete, and the slow numbered pattern below means fault. PB18 is held
off in this profile.

In a calibration fault, PA00 repeats one group every 20 seconds. A motion
feedback fault starts with one 4-second long pulse and a 1-second gap,
followed by 1-6 slow pulses matching `gMotionFault`; every count pulse is
0.6 seconds on and 0.6 seconds off. A calibration-only fault has a 1-second
gap followed by slow pulses matching `gCalibrationFault`.

1. Put the line sensor at a repeatable corner-detection position, aligned for
   the following straight. Press PA09. The car follows the side and stops at
   the next matching corner detection. It uses the official nominal
   centerline spacing of 982 mm.
2. Move the car to open level ground and keep it stationary. When PA00 shows
   two flashes, press PA09. IMU bias calibration runs first, then the car
   turns slowly through 360 degrees and stops automatically.
3. Put the sensor back at the exact corner-detection position. When PA00
   shows three flashes, press PA09 to creep forward. Press PA09 again exactly
   when the chassis rotation center is above the corner.

After stage 3, the complete record is written to Flash and survives power
cycling. The debugger cable is not needed during motion. Connect it only
after the car has stopped and inspect the `gCalibration...` Watch variables.
A new firmware download may erase the reserved sector, so record accepted
values in `chassis_config.h` before producing the competition HEX.

## Wiring

### Four-Motor TB6612 Allocation

- Right front/A: PWMA PB8, AIN1 PB6, AIN2 PB9
- Left front/B: PWMB PB26, BIN1 PA13, BIN2 PA12
- Left rear/C: PWMC PB12, CIN1 PA10, CIN2 PA11
- Right rear/D: PWMD PB2, DIN1 PB13, DIN2 PB3
- STBY: PB7

For a two-motor chassis, use channels A and B. Channels C and D mirror their
respective side commands.

### Quadrature Encoders

- Left A: PB23, interrupt on both edges
- Left B: PB27
- Right A: PA21, interrupt on both edges (PA18 is reserved for ROM BSL invocation)
- Right B: PB22

All encoder inputs use internal pull-ups. Change `ENCODER_LEFT_SIGN` or
`ENCODER_RIGHT_SIGN` when a wheel reports negative counts while moving
forward.

Encoder and line-sensor signals connected to the MCU must remain at or below
3.3 V. Use open-collector outputs pulled up to 3.3 V or level conversion when
a module produces 5 V logic.

### Line Sensor

The sensor is powered from 3.3 V. Its output was measured at 0.726 V on white
and 2.54 V on black, so it connects directly to the MCU. The SysConfig GPIO
inputs use no internal pull resistor. Do not power this sensor from 5 V while
its OUT pins are connected to the MCU.

- OUT1 PA26, OUT2 PA27, OUT3 PA24, OUT4 PA25
- OUT5 PB24, OUT6 PB25, OUT7 PA22, OUT8 PB20
- Black line is high; white background is low.

### ICM42688 SPI1

- SCLK PA17
- MISO/SDO PA16
- MOSI/SDA PB15
- CS PA08
- VCC 3.3 V, common GND, INT unused

SPI runs at about 500 kHz. Startup retries the device identity read and the
complete IMU initialization three times before refusing motion.
PA08 is driven high immediately after MCU GPIO initialization so the IMU chip
select cannot remain floating while the application waits for PA09.

### User I/O

- Start/stop button: PA09, active low
- Gyro status LED: PA00, active high
- Running debug LED: PB18, active high

### 15 A MOSFET Load Switch

- MCU PB16 -> either one of the module's two equivalent signal/PWM pins
- MCU GND -> module control-header GND pin
- Leave the other equivalent signal pin unconnected.
- Load supply positive -> module power-positive screw terminal
- Load supply negative -> module power-negative/GND screw terminal
- Load -> the two load-output screw terminals shown in the module diagram

The module is an active-high, low-side, one-direction switch. It is for fans,
pumps, lamps, heaters, and solenoids; it cannot reverse a motor. The load is
powered from its own correctly rated supply, while the MCU and load supply
must share GND. Never feed the load voltage into PB16.

The official specification states: load voltage below 36 V DC, trigger input
3-20 V, PWM input 0-20 kHz, and 15 A/400 W continuous output at room
temperature (up to 30 A only with auxiliary cooling). PB16's 3.3 V high level
is therefore within the specified trigger range. Current capability still
depends on the terminal, wire, PCB and MOSFET temperature under the real load.

The official schematic does not include a flyback diode across the load. For
an electromagnet, add one directly across the coil/output: diode cathode to
VOUT+, diode anode to VOUT-. Select a diode rated for at least the coil current
and reverse voltage. Without it, turn-off voltage can damage the MOSFET or
disturb/reset the MCU.

`power_switch_set(true)` gives full output; `power_switch_set_duty(percent)`
gives 100 Hz software PWM; `power_switch_off()` stops immediately. Call
`power_switch_update_1ms()` from the scheduler. The current application does
this automatically. Output defaults off and the configured continuous-on
timeout is 30 seconds. After a timeout, call `power_switch_off()` before a new
nonzero command; repeated ON commands cannot bypass the timeout latch.

For an electromagnet action, prefer a one-shot command such as
`power_switch_pulse_ms(500U)`. It applies full power for 500 ms and then turns
off automatically; requested pulse time is capped by the 30-second safety
limit. Call it once when entering the mission state, not repeatedly each tick.

With the default 10 ms PWM period and 1 ms scheduler tick, useful duty steps
are 10 percent. Use 10, 20, ... 100; smaller increments are rounded down by
the time base. Full ON/OFF control is preferred for solenoids and relays.

For a bench test, set `POWER_SWITCH_TEST` to `1` in `app_config.h`. All motors
remain stopped; PB16 is active only while PA09 is held. Start with a small,
fused load and set the macro back to `0` after the test.

Official module reference:
http://wiki.waaax.cn/MODULE/MOSFET15A/MOSFET15A.html

## Three-Loop Square Mission

1. Straight: line PID creates a left/right wheel-speed difference.
   The corner detector is armed only after the sensor has cleared the starting
   corner, so the start marker cannot be mistaken for the next corner.
2. Wheel speed: two encoder PI loops convert wheel-speed targets to PWM.
3. Corner approach: distance PID moves the chassis center from the detected
   intersection to the configured corner-center offset and settles there.
4. Turn: IMU heading PID commands opposite wheel speeds for a controlled
   90-degree turn; encoder speed PI remains active underneath. The task uses
   turn-relative heading magnitude so either physical gyro-Z polarity stops
   at 90 degrees, and a 5-second timeout prevents continuous spinning.
5. Reacquire: center sensors confirm the outgoing line. If needed, the IMU
   loop scans a small angle around 90 degrees; timeout stops in fault state.
6. Four corners complete one square mission.

The mission starts only while at least one center sensor sees the black line.
Seven slow LED pulses mean the start line was not centered or the mission lost
the line for 300 ms without a more specific 1-6 motion feedback fault.
Three 0.8-second header flashes followed by a numbered group identify an IMU
initialization error. One 4-second header followed by a numbered group
identifies a motion/encoder error. A 7+7 group without either header is the
line-start or line-loss fault.
The installed sensor harness maps X1 to the physical right side; reversing
the sensor board requires changing `TRACK_X1_IS_LEFT` rather than changing
PID gain signs.

The initial PID and geometry values are compile-safe starting points, not
final chassis calibration. Follow `TUNING_GUIDE.md` before enabling the
square profile on the floor.

## Keil Watch Variables

- Line: `gTrackRawMask`, `gTrackBlackMask`, `gTrackActiveCount`,
  `gTrackError`
- Encoders: `gEncoderLeftCount`, `gEncoderRightCount`,
  `gEncoderLeftSpeed`, `gEncoderRightSpeed`
- Motor output: `gLeftPwmCommand`, `gRightPwmCommand`
- IMU: `gImuReady`, `gImuReadOk`, `gImuError`, `gHeadingMdeg`
- Mission: `gMissionState`, `gMissionCornerCount`,
  `gMissionDistanceError`, `gMissionHeadingError`
- MOSFET switch: `gPowerSwitchDutyPercent`, `gPowerSwitchOutputOn`,
  `gPowerSwitchTimedOut`
- Motion safety: `gMotionFault` (zero is healthy; nonzero stops the motors)
- Physical wheel speed: `gEncoderLeftSpeedMmps`,
  `gEncoderRightSpeedMmps`
- Calibration status: `gCalibrationState`, `gCalibrationFault`,
  `gCalibrationStoredValid`,
  `gCalibrationFlags`, `gCalibrationSequence`
- Calibration results: `gCalibrationCountsPerMeter`,
  `gCalibrationEffectiveTrackMm`, `gCalibrationCornerOffsetMm`, plus the
  left/right raw count and IMU-heading variables prefixed `gCalibration`
